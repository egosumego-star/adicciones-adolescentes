Thanks to the work of Sara Cremades González (teacher and philologist), we can offer this article in English.

According to the latest results of the EDADES survey, teenagers first use alcohol and tobacco before the age of 14, and cannabis before 15.

It is scientifically proven that the younger they start, the greater the potential for harm and addiction.

The greed of some adults, who don't care about our children's lives as long as they line their own pockets, together with the hormones that push teenagers to experiment with new sensations, will make their use easier.

But the most important question is:

**Why do some get hooked and others don't?**

There are contributing factors:

- *Genetics*: some people get "hooked" on any substance incredibly easily.
- *Family*: if a parent, sibling, grandparent, etc. uses, it will open the door for the young person to do so.
- *Social hypocrisy*: on the one hand it legislates with common sense, based on serious research, and then socially it plays down the destructive effects.
- *How easy it is to get hold of the substance*: despite the efforts of the justice system and the police.
- *Friends*: although the teenager chooses them, having users in the group will logically make it more likely that they end up trying some substance.
- But the most important and least known factor is realising that it is a ***symptom*** that will make the difference between experimentation and addiction: a symptom of something traumatic that happened to them when they were very young, which they have not known how to, or been able to, overcome, and which becomes a "trigger" that will stay with them for the rest of their lives.

Some of the causes that can produce this trauma are:

- *Loss of a loved one*: parents, grandparents, siblings, etc.
- *Bullying*.
- *Abuse within the family*.
- *A sense of abandonment*: parental lack of interest, foster care, adoption, etc.
- *Having been a victim* of inappropriate touching, sexual assault or rape.
- *Having been involved in an accident*: a car crash, a fall, etc.

To help these children we must not only intervene with the substance, but also with the "trigger" that made them get hooked. If we ignore it, we will be condemned to relapse.

Honestly, I have to confess that sometimes it is impossible to identify this "trigger", because the pain has buried it so deep in the mind that it cannot be found and treated.

We are not aware of how serious addictions are (alcohol, tobacco, cannabis, gambling, technology, etc.): they are a loaded weapon that young people play and experiment with, and it can go off at any moment without us parents realising the damage that may have been done.

As a father and a professional, I suggest that if you have any suspicion that something is not right in your children's lives, you turn to the health system just as naturally as you do when they have a fever or pain.

***An early assessment and intervention can prevent a life of slavery, despair and misfortune.***
