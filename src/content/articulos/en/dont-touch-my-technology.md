Thanks to the work of **Sara Cremades González** (teacher and philologist), we can offer this article in English.

Summer is coming to an end; in a few days our children's holidays will be over. During the summer they have had hardly any rules or limits as far as technology is concerned, and they have overused it until they couldn't keep their eyes open or their thumbs went numb.  
Honestly, for us adults it has been a godsend, since they have been entertained for many, many hours, avoiding the dreaded "I'm bored...". Besides, knowing that they are in their rooms gives us a great sense of "peace of mind", above all if we have caught the fears spread by the news.  
The countdown has begun, and every hour brings us closer to going back to primary and secondary school.  
If we parents do nothing, the night before the new school year starts our children will stay connected until it is time to leave for school, where they will carry on falling asleep in the corridors.  
Those of us who work in Addictive Behaviour Units know that technology is addictive, which is why we suggest some simple strategies to ease the transition between holidays and school duties:  
1. Gradually reduce the time they spend connected.  
2. It isn't good to take away all their screen time, so depending on their age a limited amount of time should be "negotiated", but always as a reward for finished work (studies, homework).  
3. Stop buying them technology, sometimes things they haven't even asked for, to fill their bedrooms (TV, games console, computer, phone, etc.), only to then demand that they don't touch it and get on with their studies.  
4. Breakfast, lunch and dinner are not only moments to eat, but also a wonderful space to talk and, above all, to listen to the young ones while looking them in the face, so we shouldn't invite mobile phones and the TV to our table.  
5. Night time is for everyone to sleep, even the phones, which we will leave charging outside the bedrooms.

Being a mother or a father isn't easy, especially when it comes to setting limits and saying no. If we leave them to their whims, they will reproach us for the rest of our lives for our lack of firmness regarding their education and their future.
