Every week, A&E departments treat minors, sometimes little more than children (11 or 12 years old), for **alcohol poisoning**, with some even ending up in an alcohol-induced coma.  
Once the parents see their child and are told that this time they were lucky and everything is fine, they breathe a sigh of relief.  
But cases where not everything is fine are becoming more and more frequent, and test after test has to be done, waiting and waiting for signs of improvement, or the child has to be admitted to intensive care.  
Unfortunately, in most cases parents do not realise that what has just happened is a symptom of something that may be far more serious.  
Most of the adolescents who come through the Addictive Behaviour Unit are extremely intelligent and articulate. It was one of them who put it to me like this: “You adults are hypocrites when it comes to alcohol. You ban buying and drinking it until 18, claiming it is **toxic** and that, logically, the younger we are the more it harms us.  
But on the day we come of age, not only does it ‘stop being **toxic**’ so that we can buy it and drink it, but we also become the main target of advertising that encourages us to drink alcohol on the pretext of sporting events, the approach of summer or social gatherings: ‘receptions’, ‘welcome cocktails’, ‘food and wine pairing experiences’, etc…”

I know that, as Spaniards, we have a tradition of accompanying everything with alcohol, from the joy of a birth to easing the pain of losing a loved one.  
I know that every now and then studies appear, widely discussed in the press, praising the protective and sometimes even “curative” power of a certain type of alcohol. Yet when the research is repeated a few months later, it turns out that the harms and risks of drinking far outweigh the supposed “benefits”, and that no longer makes the papers. [http://revistaindependientes.com/alcohol-la-piedra-filosofal-para-una-vida-eterna-feliz-y-sin-arrugas/](http://revistaindependientes.com/alcohol-la-piedra-filosofal-para-una-vida-eterna-feliz-y-sin-arrugas/)  
I know there are multinationals turning over billions, owners of the best-selling brands, whose only interest is the profit and loss account.  
I know the desperate effort that former alcoholics’ associations have to make so that their members do not relapse into an illness that destroyed their lives, especially when the holiday advertising campaigns arrive, or the “bubbles” of Christmas.

And I ask myself:  
Couldn’t we be consistent and do something similar to what has been done with tobacco?  
I know it would mean unpopular measures, as the tobacco measures were at the time, but in the medium and long term the benefits would appear:  
Fewer deaths in road accidents.  
Fewer deaths from chronic diseases caused by drinking, etc…

Coming back to minors, personally I would:  
- Report every case of **alcohol poisoning** to Social Services, so they can find out what is going on in that family.  
- Replace the fines that parents pay today with **community service for the children**, and if they are studying, I would impose it during the holidays so they could “enjoy” it then.

**As a society, we will go on paying a high price in human lives as long as we limit ourselves to “responsible drinking”.**
