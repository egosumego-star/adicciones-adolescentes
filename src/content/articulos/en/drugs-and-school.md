It is absolutely crucial to dispel the myth that private or state-subsidised schools are drug-free; as the Spanish saying goes: "they cook beans everywhere" – every house has its problems.  
What can considerably reduce the presence of drugs is the following:

- **Schools** are the first containment wall against drugs. It is irresponsible of some school management teams to reject the free prevention resources they are offered. It has been more than proven that **prevention** is the cheapest and most effective tool in this fight. Schools that do not take up these proposals have double, or even triple, the number of students with problems.
- Both my friend Nacho (psychologist at the UCA, the Addictive Behaviours Unit, in Villena) and I think that we are reaching students too late. For prevention to be more effective, **it would have to start earlier**, in the last years of primary school (5th and 6th grade), since the latest surveys tell us that, for example, by the age of 14, 52% of boys and 48% of girls have tried marijuana (ESTUDES 2016-17). We know from experience that pre-adolescence is an ideal stage for learning about these serious problems, quite unlike adolescence.
- No one is born prepared to face the issue of addictions, which is why **AMPAS** (parents' associations) have an essential role in training parents, offering courses, talks and workshops.
- **Local councils** have the social duty to provide their citizens with information about these issues from different angles: education, policing and health.
- Personally, I think something should be done about fines, since **it is neither fair nor educational that parents are the ones who pay**. I would reduce the amount and add compulsory community service for the young people themselves (to be done at weekends or during the Easter, Christmas or summer holidays).
- Finally, the last containment wall is us, the **UCAs** (Addictive Behaviours Units); when everything else has failed, we are the only ones left. We have no minimum age for treatment – the youngest have been 12 – and no maximum age – we have patients over 80. We work with the family and the patient until they are discharged, no matter how long it takes or how many times they have tried before.

Our country may not have the best system for tackling addictions, but it is certainly one of the countries where most people get involved in helping others – the most social one. Even so, **there is still a lot to do**.
