Summer is here. Parents are still analysing where their children's grades went wrong, some because they failed and others because they didn't try hard enough.  
The children, on the other hand, no longer even remember their results, nor even the school year they have just finished or are going to repeat "because they enjoyed it so much", as happened to me.

Right now our teenagers' interests are focused on: the beach, the pool, the mountains, boys, girls, girls, boys, etc. It makes sense: on the one hand, it is the time of year when they wear the fewest clothes, which leaves little to the imagination. And most importantly, from around the age of 11, nature does a wonderful job, throwing them a hormonal "party" that will turn them into physically and sexually active women and men; and this is where the problem lies.  
Even if we parents don't like it, we need to know that our young people have their first sexual relations at around 14, so if we know the age at which it starts, the logical thing would be to have talked to them beforehand.  
We don't know what the future holds for them, whether they will be shop assistants, doctors, architects, shoe-factory workers, etc., but what we do know, with almost complete certainty, is that one day they will have sex.  
I know that we parents find it terribly embarrassing to talk to them about this, and we come up with all sorts of excuses not to: they're still very young, I'm sure they explained it at school, they know everything from the Internet, their friends will have told them, etc. But like it or not, **it is the parents' responsibility to have that conversation.**  
Ideally, it should be whichever parent is most in tune with the child at that moment who talks to them.  
My advice is to bring a couple of condoms to the conversation, one so they can learn how to put it on and another for when they need it.  
I promise you their face will light up when you begin the conversation with something like: "I think you're very young, but I know that sooner or later you'll have your first sexual relationship, and I'd like you to be prepared when the time comes…".  
If, having said that, you haven't fainted yet, you'll notice that their face is a mixture of surprise, pride and trust, because we are treating them as adults with a new responsibility: their sexuality.  
If you want top marks in this "subject", I recommend:  
- Don't even think about asking them whether they are still virgins.  
- Tell them where in the house they can find more condoms; they surely already know, but this way you'll be giving them permission to take them if they need them.

Happy summer!!
