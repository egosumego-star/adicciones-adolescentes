The Internet and its technologies have been entering our homes, our work and our social relationships (banks, public institutions, etc.) without us even realising it.

The Internet has been around for about 30 years, connected game consoles for about 20, and the first smartphones went on sale about 15 years ago.

In view of all this, three camps emerged:

1. The technology deniers, who only saw the threat of having to change their habits. Their motto was something like: "I'd rather live worse than improve my life by being forced to adapt."
2. Those open to the future, who jumped on the wave of new technologies right from the start.
3. A third group, the largest, which has simply watched, letting itself be carried along by advertising, social pressure or fashion, and which has kept buying technology (game consoles, mobile phones) without knowing absolutely anything about what it was putting into the hands of its youngest, most insistent and most astute offspring.

This last state of mental numbness has been fuelled by the constant bombardment of the news, which repeatedly shows all the "evils" that threaten our children on the streets: rape, drug-facilitated assault, beatings, drugs, etc.

So when our young people decided not to leave their rooms, they gave us a breath of false peace of mind, as we thought that at home they would always be safe.

But one day we realise that:

1. They have stopped socialising with their friends and classmates.
2. They no longer enjoy the physical activity they used to be passionate about (football, ballet, karate, athletics, etc.).
3. They come out of their rooms less and less, and if we let them, they have breakfast, lunch and dinner shut away in there.
4. The silence of the night is broken by the shouting of online games, or by the hours and hours they spend chatting and watching videos on social media.
5. As a result of these sleepless nights, when morning comes there is no way to get them out of bed to go to school.
6. At school they behave like decorative objects, because they are still asleep, even though their eyes are almost open. Logically, their falling grades reflect the time they are actually awake in class.
7. Around midday they start to wake up, not because they have slept enough, but because they are longing for the unfinished game, or for new conversations and videos.
8. In this context, a mother asked her son: "Why don't you do sport or go out with your friends any more?", to which the astute teenager replied: "All my mates have started drinking and taking drugs, so I'd rather stay at home."

We do not realise that those obedient and loving beings of a few years ago have become super-intelligent people (adolescence is the time of greatest learning potential in a human being's life), and that they have us manipulated and hooked by the... heart. Our only tool is experience, but what experience? As parents, we have none in this field.

###### **It is clear that all of us (parents, teachers, health professionals and politicians) have the responsibility to learn so that we can accompany them into this uncertain future.**
