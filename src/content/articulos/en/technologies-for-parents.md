For some years now, we parents have been living with a complex about the arrival of New Technologies (NT). We see how quickly our children master them, while we look on in amazement at their skills.  
At the same time, news keeps appearing about crimes committed using these technologies, which worries us even more when we think that our children could fall victim to some unscrupulous person.  
As we don't know what to do, all we can do is observe and watch out for any unusual behaviour in our offspring.  
And as if that weren't enough, we need to bear in mind that with the arrival of adolescence everything is new to them: their body, their way of thinking, their friends. Naturally, they are going to behave in a completely different way from how they have behaved until now, which is why we parents end up more than a little disoriented.![](http://www.adiccionesadolescentes.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/IMG-20170810-WA0012-1024x648.jpg)  
Is this a new problem? Or is it really "the same old problem" with a new name?  
For example: when they bought us our first ball, bike, motorbike, etc., **they never handed it over without giving us some Rules for using it**:  
- If you kick the ball in the middle, it will go straight.  
- If you kick it underneath, it will rise, etc.  
But they also set us clear **Limits**:  
- You'll only play once you've finished your chores, homework, etc.  
- Always outside the house and in places where there's no risk.  
- Etc.

I wonder: why don't we use this same system of rules and limits for technologies?  
It could be something like this. **Rules**:  
- The owner of the technology (computer, mobile phone, tablet, games console, etc.) is whoever pays for it, that is, the parents.  
- The access codes, both for the device and for any of the apps, will be known to the owners (the parents).  
- The device will only be used once obligations are done: homework, tidying up, washing, etc., and at the agreed times and for the agreed length of time.  
- All gadgets will be charged at night and outside the bedroom.  
Some **Limits** could be:  
- An account will never be opened, nor personal details given to anyone, without the knowledge of the device's owners.  
- They will never be used to insult, mock or harass anyone. If I am the one being harassed, I will tell my parents.  
- If someone asks me to send photos wearing few clothes, I will also tell my parents.  
- Etc.  
Finally: if the Rules and Limits are not respected, whatever was bought will be taken away.  
– Before buying anything, we must explain the conditions to our children, so as to avoid spending money if they don't agree. –

Final tips:  
1. Let's never buy them a mobile phone "so we can always reach them", because in most cases it will be "out of battery" or "out of signal", in other words, unreachable.  
2. Let's never give them one because "all" their friends have one, because next will come the piercings and tattoos.

###### ***There are no solutions easier to apply than the tried-and-tested ones***
