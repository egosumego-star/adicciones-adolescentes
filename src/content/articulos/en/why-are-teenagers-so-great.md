Since ancient times, teenagers have been torn to shreds:  
Around 2,500 years ago, Socrates said: "The young people of today are tyrants. They contradict their parents, gobble their food and disrespect their teachers."  
Somewhat younger, the physician Hippocrates added: "The young people of today seem to have no respect whatsoever for the past and no hope at all for the future."  
Nowadays they are only talked about when they get up to mischief or commit a crime. This has created a social feeling of rejection towards them.  
Many parents say that having a teenager at home is as if their affectionate, obedient child had been abducted by aliens, who returned a larger being: always angry, always asking for things, silent, playing the victim and unhappy with their body.  
If nobody taught us how to be parents, we are even more lost when we have to feed, look after and raise a "Martian" – sorry! – a teenager.  
Politicians pay them no attention, above all because these young people carry no electoral weight: they don't vote.  
The education system has a glaring problem with school failure, despite the excellent professionals working in education.  
The healthcare system doesn't know them:  
- They are the healthiest population group.  
- They have not been studied at university.  
- There is hardly any contact with them, apart from the odd consultation or a visit to A&E.

But the statistics contradict this bad reputation, since fewer than 20% of young people will have problems during adolescence; in other words, 80% will go through this stage without making much noise.  
People like **Albert Einstein, Charles Darwin, Winston Churchill, Thomas Edison, Giuseppe Verdi, Picasso, Leonardo Da Vinci, Steve Jobs, Bill Gates** and many more… belonged to that 20% of rebels without a cause: nonconformist, defiant, critical of the system; yet over time they became drivers of change and role models for all of Humanity.

***I know it won't be an easy task for anyone, but if we are able to see and support the best in them, we will not only be preventing their marginalisation, but also betting on everyone's future, because many of these teenagers will be decisive tomorrow for 100% of the population***.
