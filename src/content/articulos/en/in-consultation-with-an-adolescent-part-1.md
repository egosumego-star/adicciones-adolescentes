*Translation by Sara Cremades González (teacher and philologist).*

If you would like to listen to the article in the author's voice (in Spanish):

Antonio or Clara (fictitious names) have to go to an appointment at the Addictive Behaviours Unit this morning. The truth is that in most cases they have no idea where they are going, and even less about what is done there... They are convinced it is just another exaggeration from their parents who, living in another "era", don't know the "benefits" of smoking a few joints throughout the day, as the websites dedicated to selling marijuana so clearly explain.

What they know for sure is that nobody is going to "brainwash" them, as their parents have been trying to do for months. The truth is that just thinking about it makes them nervous, to the point that they need a joint to calm down and forget.

Other "brainwashers" are their teachers, who don't understand that every day they follow less of what is said in class, and that they often need to smoke another joint to "relax" before going into school so as not to get stressed in class. There are teachers who ignore them and leave them in peace, so they can drift off into daydreams; but there are others who insist on bothering them and asking questions about what is being discussed in class. What class? They don't even know which subject the teacher is talking about. And of course, there comes a moment when, no matter how much the teacher insists, they are not going to learn something that doesn't interest them and that they are sure they will never need; then they explode and decide not to go back to the classroom, and later not to go back to school at all.

They don't like being at home either, because their parents often argue constantly, insult each other and sometimes even come to blows; the reason is almost always the same: "there is NO money". In the end the father usually goes off to the bar and the mother stays at home crying with rage and helplessness.

Their group of friends has also changed: they no longer hang out with the kids closest to them in their neighbourhood or with their classmates, as they have already repeated a year several times. There is only one group that accepts them without questions or reproaches, where there are others like them who don't like studying or going home: the cannabis users...

It is also common for them to arrive with a personal history – despite their young age – of mistreatment, bullying or even abuse, which is rarely known to their families and therefore still unresolved.

**When they come in, they carry so much pressure in their lives that I think their addiction to marijuana, mobile phones or gambling is an escape valve to keep from going mad or making an attempt on their own life, although for some it is not enough, and they do try...**
