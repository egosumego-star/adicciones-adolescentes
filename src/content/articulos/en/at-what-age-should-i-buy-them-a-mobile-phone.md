This is the question that always comes up in talks for parents or professionals.

It is as if the only risky contact with technology happened when we buy them their mobile phone. We forget that they have been exposed to it practically since birth – for example, when we "park" a child under three in front of a mobile phone, tablet or television so that they leave us in peace for a while.

And so it goes, again and again, throughout their childhood and pre-adolescence.

But the moment will come when we have to buy them a mobile phone – the million-dollar question. Personally, I believe parents need to be prepared for what lies ahead:

1.  Buying one without the parents having at least a basic notion of **how it works, its rules and limits** is like handing children a loaded weapon with which they can do a great deal of harm to themselves, as well as to others.
2.  According to the latest statistics, a third of our children aged 11 to 16 have engaged in ***sexting*** (exchanging messages/photos of a sexual nature with their peers). So they need to know that there is no such thing as safe ***sexting*** and that their photos could mark their lives tomorrow.
3.  We must be aware that today the main "academy" for learning about sexual relationships is **pornography**, which they can access on any device at any time. It is essential that we prepare them for when this happens; otherwise, their relationships will be shaped by sexist, dominant/submissive and, above all, degrading behaviour.

It is as if, when buying a car, the only thing we cared about was age. In the United States, for example, you can drive from the age of 16.

Obviously, what matters most is knowing whether they are ready to drive, and we can only know that if their family or the driving school has taught them.

We would never let them drive without having mastered the minimum knowledge of **how it works, its rules and limits**.
