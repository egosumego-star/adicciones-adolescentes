Many of the teenagers who arrive at the Addictive Behaviour Unit have been suspended from school several times or have been permanently excluded. I don't question the reasons for this measure, but what I ask myself is: **aren't we only seeing the symptoms of something much more important?**  
- It may be that the pupil doesn't have enough logical-mathematical and linguistic skills to pass, even though they are overflowing with many others.  
Fail after fail, year after year, they pile up "failures" until in the end they give up and accept that they are "no good at studying".  
- It may be that they are in a very difficult situation at home: parents separating, abuse, a family without resources, etc.  
Family stability is the lighthouse that guides teenagers; without it, all there is in their lives is disorientation, darkness and fear.  
- It may be that they have started using alcohol or marijuana. According to the latest studies, first use in our country happens at around 13, but only those who are in class are asked. If we asked those who have been expelled, I'm sure the age of onset would be different.  
The best detectors of this early use are schools and families.  
- It may be that they are being bullied, and that their repeated lateness or absences are because they don't want to run into the bullies.  
- It may be a little of each of the reasons above, but for a teenager the sum of these littles adds up to a lot.  
If, faced with everything that may be going on, the education system simply leaves them out on the street – yes, I said on the street! – because I don't think anyone believes that child is going to stay at home studying; the usual thing will be to head to parks and hangouts to be with others, often older than them.  
What's more, with every expulsion catching up with their classmates becomes harder, until there is no possibility of returning…

Those of us who work with teenagers know how hard they find it to show their emotions, so I think that, in the case of repeated or long-term expulsions, it should be mandatory for the school to ask itself: **what is going on in this pupil's life?**  
If the measure were still upheld, I think it would be advisable to bring back "**expulsion classrooms**" run by motivating teachers, so that these pupils don't disconnect from the system and can be reintegrated in the best possible conditions, so as not to swell the lists of school failure and drugs.
