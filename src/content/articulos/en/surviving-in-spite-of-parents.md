We have got used to hearing about teenagers only when they do something stupid or commit a crime, just as lately we only hear about parents when they kill a child (filicide).  
All of us parents do the best we know how, and we should never feel guilty if our offspring leave us disoriented, exhausted, angry, etc. That doesn't mean we have done it wrong or that our child has turned out "faulty".  
I remember the story of a father who was so overwhelmed by his 14-year-old son that he walked into the Unit, put him in the first consulting room he found open, and said to the professional inside: "I can't take any more! Call me when you've fixed him."  
Our children are wonderful, unique beings, and they don't come programmed to "get on our nerves" when they reach adolescence.  
Nobody can ask us to be perfect parents, because nobody has taught us, although we do have a duty to learn; but they can ask us to be consistent:  
- We can't forbid them to smoke with a cigarette in our hand.  
- We can't forbid them to drink alcohol if we drink.  
- We can't forbid them to shout if we yell at them.  
- We can't forbid them to smoke joints if we have a marijuana plant for personal use.  
- We can't ask them to be cultured people if we never get past the headlines of the sports papers.  
- We can't reach an agreement with them if we can't agree with each other as parents.  
- We can't ask them to switch off their phones if we aren't the first to do so.  
- We can't ask them to tell us things if we turn on the television at lunchtime so we don't have to listen to them.  
- We can't ask them to respect us if we treat our elders like dirt.  
- We can't ask them to study and rest in their bedroom if we fill it with tech gadgets: TV, games console, mobile phone, etc.

If we want to have responsible adults in the near future, they must learn from the example of those who would be willing to give their lives for them: their Parents.
