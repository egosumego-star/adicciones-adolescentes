It's a rare day when I don't hear the lament:

Why does my son hate me?

often accompanied by tears, guilt and helplessness.

We should bear in mind that **no one teaches us how to be parents**, even though once we decide to become one, we have a duty to learn. It is curious that the education system doesn't include this life "subject", which most of us adults will go through, and which guarantees not only the continuation of the species but also the emotional stability of both parents and children.

If we require a licence proving that someone knows the rules of the road just to ride a small moped, why don't we ask for some basic knowledge in order to be parents?

We have to show parents that **adolescence is like a volcanic eruption**, in which the child elbows their way through whatever is closest (the family), searching not only for their size but also for their identity and their place in society. This "volcano" will grow little by little until it reaches its full size, becoming a unique adult.

However much volcanoes frighten us humans, they are clearly a regenerating element of the Earth's crust; trying to control them is a utopia, but preparing ourselves for their consequences can save a great many lives.

Parents need to bear in mind that, just as teenagers are breaking in new bodies they have to learn to use day by day, **they are very clumsy with emotions**: they are unable to put them into words, and that irritates them until they explode and say things that hurt but that they don't really feel. A while later they may be asking for their afternoon snack as if nothing had happened (a true story).

**They are unable to ask for help** when something worries them, overwhelms them or frightens them (bullying, family abuse, sexual identity, etc.), or when they simply need to be listened to.

As my teacher and friend Dr. Antonio Ríos says, listening to your children is not the same as subjecting them to a police interrogation: Where have you been? What have you done? Who were you with?

Unfortunately, teenagers are the great unknowns of the system. Very often, the children's "voicelessness" plus the "deafness" of parents, teachers or health professionals ends in behavioural problems, school failure and substance use, which in a short time turn into an addiction: cannabis, technology, alcohol.

I believe there are no perfect parents, but as a society we have a duty to make the necessary resources available to them.

We just have to make them responsible for

**ASKING FOR HELP**.
