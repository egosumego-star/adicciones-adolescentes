Thanks to the work of Sara Cremades González (teacher and philologist), we can offer this article in English.

Human beings need help to survive from the moment they are born; in addition, we have the longest upbringing period of all living beings.  
But this help is especially important in mental illness and in addiction. In both cases the patient is not aware of their illness and often refuses treatment.  
I remember the case of a young boy who smoked joints and who said he was very lucky because at 12 o'clock every night he could communicate with God through his computer. The problem wasn't only who he was talking to: the computer was switched off and, as his parents told us in tears, the house didn't even have Internet.  
When we proposed treatment to him, he refused it because he believed we were trying to separate him from God. Moreover, he claimed that he wasn't harming anyone, given that all he needed to be happy was the odd joint and his conversations.  
We had to work a lot, above all with his family: first, helping them accept that although their son was ill, fortunately his illness could be treated. But for him to start treatment, we had to support the parents until the boy's resistance was overcome.  
It might seem a strange case, but there is plenty of research showing that a regular user of alcohol, cocaine, joints, etc., who has been taking these substances for some time, has more than a 60% chance of developing a mental illness.  
Health professionals know that it is essential to work with and support families, and never abandon them, since they are the ones who spend 24 hours a day with the patient, they are the ones who keep the hope of recovery alive, and they are the ones who will accompany that person for the rest of their lives.  
We health professionals will come and go, and even retire, but the family will always be there.  
I hope this simple article serves as a small tribute to all those mothers, fathers, wives, husbands and siblings who have a person with an addictive or mental illness at home.  
I don't want to finish without reminding you that 10 October is World Mental Health Day.

A kiss and a big hug to all of them.
