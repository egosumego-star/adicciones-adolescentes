Thanks to the translation done by Sara Cremades González (teacher and philologist), you can read this article in English:

**WHAT ARE WE BREEDING, ENTREPRENEURS, GENIUSES OR MELONS?**

I am convinced of the good will of parents, teachers or health professionals. In general, they all do the best they can or **the best they KNOW** and here we have the Achilles heel and at the same time a great opportunity.

Parents today, in such a demanding society, cannot limit themselves to feeding, dressing and taking their children to school, especially with the Internet and its technologies.

The education system is totally out of date and no matter how good the professionals who train it, it is not possible to teach only with the educational tools used by Fray Luis de León (16th century).

Medicine is making spectacular advances month after month, above all thanks to the support of the big pharmaceutical companies that make expensive and lengthy research possible, but what happens to the healthiest population group, the one that needs the least medication: adolescents? They are hardly ever seen in the health system, and when they do appear, it is in the emergency department.

They are the great unknown, they are uncomfortable and often unpredictable patients; in fact, they are hardly studied in university degrees in Health Sciences.

Personally, I do not believe that no one comes to solve the problems of our children, students or patients, but I am a believer in **the power of each of us** to improve the lives of others.

I am not the one to give advice, but:  

  
-   Perhaps if we parents dedicated just one hour a month to get to know the stages our children go through: childhood, puberty, adolescence; we would understand better what is happening with them at each moment and if something is not going well, we would know where and who to ask for help.
  
-   Perhaps teachers and professors with reservations or rejection of technology could look into the eyes of their students and see that these "gadgets" are part of their daily lives, and that a future without them is unthinkable. ICTs (Information and Communication Technologies) can be a great tool for teaching, knowing that "a screwdriver will never replace a mechanic, just as technology will never replace a good teacher".
  
-   Perhaps we health professionals should look beyond the disease; **work with healthy people** to: delay or prevent disease, have the best quality of life and "perpetuate" youth, even if it is with silver temples. In a word, practising **Proactive**
