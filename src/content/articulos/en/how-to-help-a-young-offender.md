If you would like to listen to the article in the author's voice:

For some years now, professionals working with juvenile court measures have been asking me to give talks to their young people to help them get back on the right path.

I am not the only one: there is a group of professionals who gladly and selflessly collaborate in this challenge.

At first it seems an intervention doomed to failure, since we are talking to a group of minors who have just left a juvenile detention centre after committing an offence, often linked to the use of some drug. On top of that, talking to them about drugs when some of them are still using, when in some cases their family is involved in that "business", when they know no other way of life than their parents'... you'll agree with me that it is, to say the least, a tall order...

It is clear that, however many years of experience I may have, when it comes to the latest developments in narcotics (cultivation, properties and sale) they are the real experts.

And yet these are the talks I enjoy giving most. In a moment of "inspiration" I decided to focus them on their most vulnerable point: **their Future**.

Everyone, from professionals in the Education, Justice and Health systems, etc., sends them the same message over and over again: "IF YOU CARRY ON DOWN THAT ROAD, IN THE BEST-CASE SCENARIO YOU'LL END UP IN PRISON AND, IN THE WORST, YOU'LL DIE YOUNG".

When they have heard this message again and again, they end up believing it (a self-fulfilling prophecy). They start ignoring everyone who wants to help them and begin to live faster and more dangerously, given the "little time" they have left before they "go to prison or die". As a society, we get the opposite of the result we wanted.

My modest intervention consists of sending them the following message:

"**NO MATTER WHERE YOU ARE IN YOUR LIFE, IT IS IN YOUR HANDS TO MAKE IT BETTER**".

To do this, I look up the biographies of famous people they know who had a terrifying childhood or adolescence. I then tell them how their lives turned around until they became the successful people we know: Halle Berry, Michael Phelps, Leonardo DiCaprio and many others...

When I tell them these stories of abandonment, beatings, sexual abuse, etc., I can see the eyes of many of them welling up, because of their own everyday reality.

I want my message to be clear:

"**IF THEY WANT TO GET OUT, THEY WILL!**" – others have done it in worse circumstances.

But I also aim to be honest: "**IT WON'T BE QUICK OR EASY. BUT IT WILL BE WORTH IT**".
