I have never believed in "bad" children, but in children who are living through harsh circumstances and whose way of escaping to forget their daily reality is crime or drugs.  
By chance, while reading, I discovered many similarities between the biographies of successful people and my lads and lasses:

- Rebels without a cause.
- Defiant towards the established system.
- Fierce activists.
- Etc.

After this discovery, I began to treat them as if they were the next Einstein (physics genius), Barack Obama (former President of the United States) or our own Amancio Ortega (founder and owner of Zara). This made me throw myself into my work with more passion, as I saw the young people in front of me through different eyes.  
In fact, above where I sit in my office, there is a framed sign that reads: "HERE WE ONLY SEE CHAMPIONS AND GENIUSES. CHOOSE!!" It is also hung at a slight angle so that it breaks the balance with its surroundings and draws more attention.  
When we treat these young people not only with respect and dignity, but with absolute conviction in their incredible potential, the relationship changes extraordinarily, because they are used to the world kicking them around, just as they kick the world around.

- Suddenly, they go from "lying down" in the chair to sitting up straight or leaning slightly forward.
- Suddenly, they go from studying "the quality of the paint on the walls and ceiling" to opening their eyes and not taking them off our conversation.
- Suddenly, they go from a face like thunder to relaxing and starting to smile and joke.
- Suddenly, they go from total silence to talking non-stop, even when you don't ask them anything.

I remember the story of some parents who, after the first appointment with their son, I told how talkative he had been; the father asked me whether I already knew him, because he and the boy's mother couldn't manage to talk with him for more than half an hour in a whole month.  
I have always thought that if we treat our fellow human beings like beggars, we will only receive complaints and misery; whereas if we treat them like princes, their answers will be pearls of hope and diamonds of wisdom.  
That is how my colleagues and I make small advances every day, small "miracles", with the future Einsteins, Barack Obamas or Amancio Ortegas.
