If you would like to listen to the article in the author's own voice:

Every week, people with the same **problem** come to or call the Addictive Behaviours Unit in Elda (UCA):  
They have a family member (a son or daughter, a husband, a mother, etc.) whom they suspect may be using (alcohol, joints, cocaine, etc.) or abusing some technology (mobile phone, computer, games console, etc.), to the point that they are losing their job, their studies, their family, and so on. And, of course, their health.  
It is heartbreaking to listen to these relatives describe, through tears, how powerless they feel at not being able to help their loved one, as their support is rejected again and again with arguments like: "there's nothing wrong with me", "you're mad", "I'm not a drunk…", "I'm not hooked, everybody does it…".  
A few days ago Isabel's parents (not her real name) came in, very distressed about their 15-year-old girl, because since last year she had become a completely different person:  
At home she was always angry, complaining about the food, her clothes, her life… Yet when she went out, she became that kind, polite, smiling daughter again, as neighbours and relatives confirmed.  
At secondary school all her grades had dropped and she had failed several subjects.  
She had changed friends and no longer went around with her usual group.

This week Antonio's wife (a made-up name) also came to ask for help. She says her husband has always been a good, hard-working man, but since he lost his job he has never been the same. He goes from job to job, always ends up on bad terms, and most of the time says he hasn't been paid.  
At home nobody can put up with him, because he is always either sleeping or cursing.  
Both his wife and his children are frightened and bewildered.  
In calmer moments, when they try to talk to him, the answer is always the same: "I don't have any **problem**, leave me alone…!"

In both cases it is the family who has the **problem**, having noticed that something serious is happening, even though the likely cause, the **illness** (joints, alcohol, cocaine, technologies, etc.), really belongs to Isabel and Antonio.

At the unit we listen to the parents and relatives, explain what is probably going on, and once they have got their "breath" and the "colour back in their cheeks", we start suggesting strategies to get the ill person to come to the Unit.  
**We are clear that nobody will start treatment while they think they are not *ill*. What we always keep in mind in our work is that we never abandon those who suffer, those who have the *problem*, however stubbornly the *ill* person denies reality.**
