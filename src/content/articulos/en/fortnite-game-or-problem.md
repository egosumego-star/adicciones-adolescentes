*Translated by Sara Cremades González (teacher and philologist).*

Fortnite is a game with 250 million registered players. It works on any current games console (PlayStation, Wii, Xbox), as well as on any latest-generation mobile phone.  
Its dynamics are simple and easy to learn: 100 players land on an island and only one can be left standing, while the island keeps getting smaller.  
Like any online game, it can be played at any time of day in short matches that barely last 30 minutes.  
The maker (Epic Games) considers that, even though it is a death/elimination game, since you do not see any blood, it is suitable from the age of 12 (PEGI 12+).  
It is “free”: it costs nothing to start, but if you want more “fashionable” outfits (skins) or want your characters to dance and mock their opponents, then you have to pay, between €5 and €20. This is where the maker earns its money.  
Having said all this, we reach two conclusions:  
1. As a game and in its design, it is rather mediocre.  
2. It is a fantastic minimalist marketing operation (free, immediate, on any device, etc.) that gives no importance at all to the story or the graphics (they are cartoons).  

So I ask myself: who is responsible for ever younger children spending hour after hour hooked on the game?  
**The child?** – They are only doing what is fashionable, and this allows them to socialise, not only inside the game but also at school and in the street, because they can give their opinion on something they share with many of their peers.  
**The game?** – Holding the game responsible is like holding a sharp knife responsible for cutting us. Things themselves bear no responsibility.  
**The parents?** – We parents are indeed responsible for allowing our children to play anything at all. We cannot expect a minor to make an adult and responsible use of the game, since they are neither adults nor responsible.  

We cannot hand a loaded weapon to a child and expect them not to hurt themselves or others. The first thing we need to know as parents is what a weapon is.  

**At the very least, let us learn with them how to use technology (mobile phones, games consoles, etc.), let us play and enjoy their company, because when they are a little older we will miss those moments of chatting, laughing and joking.**
